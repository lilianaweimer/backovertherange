The town takes its name from a peculiar upthrust of rock on the summit of a conical hill,
resembling, in the distance, an old martelle tower, and nearer by an irregular pentagonal structure.
Under the shadow of this hill and surmounting tower lies the town, which is a pretty village and the
county seat of Douglas County. Fine quarries of red sandstone are worked here, and pastoral
industries contribute to the prosperity of the town. (Population, 300. Distance from Denver, 33
miles. Elevation, 6,219 feet.)
